# Crypto and Decentralization

**Status: Conceptual; no cryptographic architecture selected.**

Circula’s mutual-credit accounting does not require a cryptocurrency, token, blockchain, or mining. Crypto can refer to cryptography: digital signatures, encryption, and tamper-evident records. These tools might support verifiable approvals and portability, but require safe key recovery and can create privacy and usability risks.

The crypto-libertarian design goal is voluntary networks with local governance, self-hosting options, consent-based interoperability, and credible exit. Decentralization distributes control and responsibility; it does not remove governance or guarantee privacy.

A conventional transactional database is the proposed starting point for atomic balanced postings. Public-chain anchors, federation, signatures, or credentials would need a defined user problem and separate threat model. See [the detailed exploration](../docs/brainstorming/crypto-and-crypto-libertarian-design.md).
